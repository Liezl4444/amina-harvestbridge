Founding Story

HarvestBridge was founded in 2011 by Amina Mokoena and Sipho Dlamini in response to a structural problem both had witnessed working in agricultural development in Limpopo: smallholder farmers were producing high-quality food but capturing almost none of its value. Retailers and distributors took the margin; producers took the risk.

The founding model was a cooperative aggregation platform — bringing 340 smallholder families into a single supply network, collectively negotiating contracts, sharing logistics costs, and maintaining individual farm traceability throughout the chain. The cooperative's founding charter explicitly tied commercial success to SDG 2: food security and fair income for small-scale producers.

By 2016 the cooperative was profitable and had attracted national retail listings. The founding purpose had held — but the ceiling was visible. Scaling required capital and manufacturing infrastructure neither founder could provide alone.

The Scale Journey — Three Levels of Innovation

  • M
    2011–2016 · Macro Scale
    The Limpopo Cooperative

    340 smallholder farming families aggregated into a single traceable supply network. Founding SDG 2 mission intact. Profitable, growing, but capital-constrained. The cooperative is still the moral foundation of everything HarvestBridge does — Amina describes it as "the reason we exist."

  • M
    2017–2023 · Meso Scale
    Ambient Food Manufacturing (Meridian Capital partnership)

    Private equity investment from Meridian Capital enabled the construction of a 4,200m² ambient food manufacturing facility in Polokwane. Artisanal cooperative products were standardised for national retail at scale. Revenue grew from R62m to R380m over six years. The cooperative was restructured from founding partner to preferred supplier — a transition that was efficient, and contested.

  • m
    2024–present · Micro Scale
    HarvestTable — Sandton Flagship

    A premium experiential retail concept: locally sourced, traceable, story-driven food for Sandton's urban affluent consumer. The concept was developed with a specialist chef-consultant and opened in March 2024. Early reviews were strong. Current performance is below target. A board meeting is scheduled for next week. Meridian Capital wants a replication plan or an exit strategy.

Purpose & Values

HarvestBridge operates under an explicit SDG 2 commitment — zero hunger, food security, improved nutrition, and sustainable agriculture. The original cooperative charter remains the company's founding document; it has never been formally revised, though its practical weight in board decisions has diminished since the Meridian partnership.

The company's four stated values are: Traceability (every product carries a producer story), Reciprocity (commercial success returns value to the communities that make it possible), Integrity (what we say about our sourcing is what we do), and Ambition (purpose does not mean small — we intend to scale).

The tension between Ambition and Reciprocity — between growth and the original cooperative promise — is the defining strategic challenge of HarvestBridge's current moment.

Chief Executive
AM

Amina Mokoena

Chief Executive Officer · Co-Founder

Amina Mokoena co-founded HarvestBridge in 2011 following a decade in agricultural development across Limpopo and Mpumalanga. She holds an MBA from GIBS (2009) and a BSc Agricultural Economics from the University of Pretoria. She has led the company through three strategic transformations — from cooperative, to manufacturing operation, to premium retail experiment.

She is known for precision in communication and a strategic composure that colleagues describe as reassuring in a crisis and occasionally frustrating in a normal week. She has twice been named to the Mail & Guardian 200 Young South Africans list. She sits on the board of the Limpopo Agricultural Development Trust.

In 2023 she was a founding investor in an SA-based food distribution venture. The full extent of her portfolio interests has not been disclosed in her HarvestBridge board declaration.

Tebogo joined HarvestBridge in 2018 from a large-scale FMCG logistics operation in Durban. He oversees the Polokwane manufacturing facility, supply chain procurement, and logistics across all three divisions. He is widely credited with the efficiency gains that took group EBITDA from 4.1% to 9.2% between 2019 and 2023.

Human Resources Director

Nadia has been with HarvestBridge since 2015, joining from a mining HR practice in Rustenburg. She oversees talent, culture, and people strategy across the group's 214 permanent staff and approximately 80 seasonal contract workers.

She holds an Honours degree in Industrial Psychology from the University of the Witwatersrand. She is described by colleagues as meticulous and, by some, as persistent to the point of discomfort when she believes something needs to be heard.

Human Resources

Priya joined in 2022 to lead the brand strategy for the HarvestTable concept. She designed the traceability storytelling model that underpins HarvestTable's consumer proposition.

Her team of six includes three contract consultants. Two of those consultants have been in informal conversations with a European food company that recently announced entry into the South African premium food segment.

Co-Founder & Stakeholder
SD

Sipho Dlamini

Community Liaison · Smallholder Representative

Sipho Dlamini co-designed the original HarvestBridge cooperative model with Amina in 2011. For the cooperative's first six years, he held the title of Operations Director and served as the primary relationship manager for all 340 farming families in the Limpopo network.

In 2019, following the Meridian Capital investment and the associated governance restructure, Sipho's role was reclassified as Community Liaison. He retains a seat on the cooperative's advisory committee but no longer holds a formal directorship or equity stake in the manufacturing entity. He continues to represent smallholder interests in supplier contract negotiations.

Co-Founder
In September 2025, a formal complaint was filed by the Limpopo Smallholder Collective — of which Sipho is a member — regarding the terms of the current cooperative supply contract. The complaint is under review by HarvestBridge's legal team. It does not appear on the upcoming board agenda.
Investor
MC

Meridian Capital

Private Equity Investor · 41% Stake

Meridian Capital is a Johannesburg-based mid-market PE fund specialising in agri-food and consumer goods. They led a R120m Series B investment into HarvestBridge in 2017, enabling the construction of the Polokwane manufacturing facility.

Their current board representative is Marcus van der Berg (Managing Partner, Meridian Capital). Meridian's investment mandate is a 7–9 year horizon, targeting a 3× return on invested capital via trade sale or listing. They have been patient investors. At the next board meeting, patience is understood to be a constrained resource.
