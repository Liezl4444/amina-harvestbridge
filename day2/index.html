About HarvestBridge

HarvestBridge began as a smallholder aggregation cooperative in Limpopo, built on a founding mission aligned with SDG 2: food security and fair income for small-scale producers. Co-founded by Amina Mokoena and Sipho Dlamini, the company has grown into one of South Africa's most ambitious agri-food enterprises — but the growth has not been without cost.

A private equity partnership with Meridian Capital (41% stake) funded the transition to ambient food manufacturing. The cooperative became a supplier contract. The artisanal became industrial. And now, HarvestTable — a premium Sandton retail concept opened in March 2024 — is underperforming at a moment when the board wants answers.

Mission: Nourishing communities, connecting growth. The question for this board meeting: what does that mission actually mean for the decisions ahead?

The Three Innovation Scales

  • M

    Macro — The Limpopo Cooperative

    The founding tier. 340 registered smallholder farmers. Currently supplying 68% of HarvestBridge's manufacturing inputs — but that figure is under quiet review. A competing aggregator, Agri-Connect SA, has been making contact with cooperative members. Sipho Dlamini called Amina last night.

  • M

    Meso — Ambient Food Manufacturing

    The commercial backbone. R388m revenue (FY2025). Carries the group's EBITDA. Meridian's investment thesis was built here. Staff turnover in this division has climbed from 14% to 22% over two years. The HR Director has flagged this twice. Both times it appeared as a footnote.

  • M

    Micro — HarvestTable, Sandton

    The retail hypothesis. Premium, traceable, story-driven food for urban consumers. NPS: 31. Margin: –4.1%. The specialist chef-consultant who designed the menu is gone. Dr Priya Naidoo has a "soil to shelf" campaign ready — but Amina hasn't approved it, because she's not yet sure the store will still exist.
