🧭 Three Strategic Directions on the Table

Amina has prepared three options for tomorrow's board. Each carries a different theory of value, a different model of growth, and a different answer to the question: what is HarvestBridge really for?

1
Double Down on Sandton

Fix HarvestTable. Find the right executive chef — someone who can turn the store into a destination, not just a retail shelf. Invest in the brand experience, renegotiate the Sandton lease, and build the story that Meridian originally funded: premium African food innovation in a flagship format. The manufacturing base funds the experiment until HarvestTable breaks even.

Capital intensive Execution risk high Meridian alignment Christensen: sustaining innovation
2
Return to Roots

Exit retail. Close or divest HarvestTable and recommit entirely to the cooperative and manufacturing core. Rebuild the cooperative supplier share back to 85%+, invest in Limpopo farmer capacity, and pursue B-Corp certification. Reframe the Meridian story: not a premium retail play, but Africa's most trusted agri-food manufacturer supplying institutional buyers — hospitals, universities, corporate canteens.

Purpose alignment Margin recovery Meridian may exit Louw & Jordaan: systems thinking
3
Non-Disruptive Creation

Neither fix Sandton nor abandon it — create a new market segment that didn't previously exist. Pursue dual Rainforest Alliance and Fair Trade certification, giving HarvestBridge access to European and East Asian premium import markets. The cooperative becomes the origin story for a certified ethical supply chain. HarvestTable pivots to a trade and wholesale hub for B2B buyers, not a consumer retail experience.

New revenue stream Cooperative protected 18–24 month timeline Kim & Mauborgne: blue ocean

These are Amina's options — not her decision. She hasn't told the board which one she recommends. Ask her why when you speak to her.
